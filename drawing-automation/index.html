Workflow automation

Drawing Automation

From reinforcement drawings
to field-ready instructions.

I automated repetitive drafting and spreadsheet work during my engineering internship, translating supplier information into the drawings and material records used by the construction team.

Context
Chuan Gao · 2023–2024
Tools
AutoLISP · Excel VBA
Outcome
Tools remained in production use

01 / The input

Detailed drawings.
Repeated translation.

Supplier reinforcement drawings carry bar marks, sizes, shapes, lengths and quantities. The crew needs another view: where to work, which bars to place, and how to assemble each beam or column.

The automation reorganized that information around the crew’s drawing conventions, reducing repeated manual redrafting.

02 / The translation

Keep the data.
Change how it is read.

The workflow identified information in supplier drawings, organized it as structured data, then used AutoLISP to draw it in a format familiar to the crew.

Supplier notation

B3510–D25L = 7810 mm

Read the bar group and its associated dimensions.

Structured record

{
  "bar_mark": "B35",
  "quantity": 10,
  "source_size": "D25",
  "cut_length_mm": 7810,
  "shape": "A2",
  "segments_mm": [350, 7460]
}

Separate the attributes before placing the drawing elements.

Crew-facing notation

B35Bar group

10 × #8

Cut length781 cm

D25 → #8 · Convert the notation and place the labels.

AutoLISP places the bar geometry and annotations using the crew’s drawing layout.

Illustrative workflow. B35 values are transcribed from the supplied drawing; the JSON record and crew layout are reconstructed. Bar shape is schematic, not to scale.

Match the member

Keep the floor, member ID and bar mark distinct. A grouped quantity must not become a per-member quantity.

Use the crew’s language

Show # bar sizes, bar counts, stirrup forms and spacing, with lap, hook and coupler details where required.

Carry the engineering detail

Lap lengths, hooks and connection locations come from the project drawings and approved requirements.

03 / The output

A location plan.
A sheet for each member.

The plan tells the crew where to work. Individual beam and column sheets bring the reinforcement instructions together, rather than leaving them scattered across the supplier drawing.

Alongside the drawings

Turn dimensions
into material quantities.

Spreadsheet work organized dimensions and counts into material lists and pricing preparation. This supplied steel-mesh workbook shows the quantity calculation: specification lookup, weight totals, loss allowance and standard-panel demand.

My contribution

Automate the repeated work.
Keep the engineering intent.

I developed AutoLISP and Excel VBA tools for drafting and spreadsheet tasks during my site engineering internship. The tools remained in production use after the internship.

The workflow and layouts here are reconstructed from supplied reference drawings, a workbook and recollection. Original scripts and generated CAD files are currently unavailable.
