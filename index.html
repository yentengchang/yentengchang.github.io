Structural Engineering × AI × Systems Integration

Research Assistant · Civil Engineering · National Taiwan University

I work across structural engineering, structural health monitoring, computer vision, edge AI, spatial computing, and applications of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs).

My goal is to understand structural condition and behavior through multimodal sensing and computational analysis, and use that understanding to support intelligent assessment, engineering workflow automation, and decisions about maintenance, retrofitting, and other interventions.

Featured project · Sinotech R&D

Auto Inspector

An iPad inspection workflow that connects detected defects to the rooms, surfaces and coordinates where they belong.

Sole developer · 2025–2026

From site to spatial recordExplore the workflow ↓

01 · One inspection workspace

Give every observation a place.

Photos, measurements and sketches are difficult to reconcile after leaving a site. Auto Inspector brings spatial capture and defect records into one iPad workflow.

02 · RoomPlan + ARKit

Build the room as you move.

Walls, openings and objects become a dimensioned spatial model. The scan supplies the shared coordinate frame that the inspection will use.

03 · CeilingMeshExtractor

Include what is overhead.

A room is more than its walls. Custom ARKit mesh processing extends the reconstruction to ceiling and upper-level surfaces.

04 · Detection → spatial record

Move beyond a box on an image.

Tap a detected region to project it onto a reconstructed surface. The photograph, camera pose and 3D coordinates remain linked for later review.

05 · Review and export

Keep the evidence in context.

Review defect markers in the measured 3D model, then open the dimensioned floor plan. Images, spatial records and exports preserve the same inspection context.

06 · Field operation

From one room to a working site.

Repeated field tests covered studio apartments, multi-room residences, special-use buildings and construction sites. These recordings show the software in use.

Working-App interface reconstruction with recorded room geometry. The moving scan backdrop is reference footage; ceiling layer and projection are illustrative. Anchoring and workflow videos are actual recordings.

Featured research · Master’s thesis

Cross-Architecture Knowledge Distillation

Knowledge distillation for efficient, on-device defect inspection.

Research design & implementation · 2024–2026

Better defect detection on compact hardware

Distillation improves a lightweight detector without enlarging the deployed model. Cross-architecture transfer lets different teachers train that student, without a fixed teacher–student pairing.

Controlled experiments to validate the method

Multiple experimental and control configurations each run with the same five seeds, separating design effects from variation between training runs.

Foreground weighting for thin defects

Uniform, box-derived and mask weighting compare where teacher supervision acts. The mask concentrates that supervision along the defect.

Distillation strength as a separate control

Foreground weighting also changes the loss magnitude. Stronger uniform distillation provides a control for that increase, separating supervision strength from spatial weighting.

A control for the added loss

Shuffled targets retain the extra loss but disrupt image correspondence, testing whether useful teacher information contributes beyond regularization.

Recovering 17% of the teacher–student gap

No added inference parameters or operations.

Featured project · Sanlien Technology Corp.

Real-Time Crack Monitoring and Measurement System

A multi-camera Windows system that converts continuous crack imagery into measurements in physical units and event-linked records.

Sole developer · September 2025 – January 2026

System walkthrough Scroll to explore ↓

Real-time camera feed

Monitor each crack through a live, high-resolution stream.

The incoming camera feed, detection controls and measurement results remain together in one operational view for continuous monitoring.

Physical scale

Translate pixels into physical dimensions.

A checkerboard with a known square size establishes the pixel-to-millimeter scale used for every subsequent measurement.

Instance segmentation

Move from detection to precise crack boundaries.

Instance segmentation delineates each crack for physical measurement. Multiple cracks can be detected and tracked in real time, preserving a separate measurement history for each one.

Physical crack length

Convert the skeletonized mask into physical length.

Skeletonization reduces the segmented crack to its centerline. Combined with the established pixel-to-millimeter scale, it yields the crack’s physical length.

Maximum crack width

Measure the widest point in physical units.

The distance transform evaluates width along the full crack, then records the maximum physical width—a key value for engineering assessment.

Multi-camera monitoring

Extend the same workflow across camera streams.

The single-camera view becomes a coordinated monitoring wall for concurrent observation.

Seismic event recording

The record begins before the trigger.

A rolling buffer preserves the lead-up, then continues through and after the seismic event—organizing every camera stream under the same event record.

Featured project · Inspection R&D

Lock-Up Device Intelligent Inspection

From visible deterioration to engineering assessment. A workflow connecting RGB-D measurement, standards-constrained AI, and knowledge from engineer-reviewed cases.

Sole developer · Research & system development

System walkthrough Scroll to explore ↓

Field inspection

Bring the field into one workspace.

Lock-up devices connect bridge components and restrain rapid movement. The inspection workflow brings field imagery, depth data, and device records together for engineering review.

Deterioration segmentation

Make deterioration visible, region by region.

Pixel-level regions separate oil leakage, corrosion, and coating loss. Each region becomes a piece of evidence that can be measured and assessed.

RGB-D surface measurement

Give each region a physical extent.

A depth camera adds distance to the image. Calibrated depth and camera intrinsics recover the surface in 3D; the deterioration mask defines the region to measure for DERU extent.

Pin-to-pin measurement

Two selected points. A distance in 3D.

The operator selects both pin centres in the field image. In the RGB-D workflow, their depth and camera geometry support pin-to-pin measurement.

Standards-constrained VLM

Constrain what the model can conclude.

The vision–language model selects from matched inspection criteria, within an allowed grade set and a fixed output schema. It suggests D; geometry determines E, while R and U remain with the engineer.

Validation & human review

Check the output. Keep the engineer in control.

Schema, rule, and quality checks gate the candidate assessment. Uncertain cases go to an engineer, with the initial suggestion, correction, and reason kept together.

Reviewed-case knowledge

Make each review useful beyond this inspection.

The reviewed grade, supporting image, and engineer’s rationale become a retrievable case. A correction adds context to the knowledge base—not just another archive entry.

Reviewed-case RAG

Bring engineering judgement into the next assessment.

Relevant reviewed cases return alongside inspection standards as context for the next VLM assessment. The same output constraints and validation still apply.
