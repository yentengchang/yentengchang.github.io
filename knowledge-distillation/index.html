Different teachers.
One chosen student.

A compact crack-and-spalling detector should benefit from a stronger teacher without being tied to one model pair.

The transfer interface uses detector outputs: compatible head distributions or cached pseudo-labels from another architecture.

Two tested teachers, one student. The routes are alternatives—not an ensemble.

Inside the two transfer paths

Compatible detection heads

YOLO11l → YOLO11n. A frozen online teacher supplies temperature-scaled sigmoid class targets for binary cross-entropy and 16-bin box distributions for KL divergence. Temperature: 2.

Class, spatial-grid and box-bin semantics must be compatible. Foreground weighting applies to this route.

Cached pseudo-labels

RT-DETR-L → YOLO11n. Boxes, classes and confidence above 0.5 form an additional detection loss (CIoU, classification and distribution focal loss), scaled by batch-mean confidence and λ = 0.5.

Ground-truth supervision is retained. Valid coordinates and class mapping are required; geometric augmentation is disabled to preserve cached-box correspondence.

Controlled comparisons validate the design.

Each configuration changes a specific part of the supervision. Every configuration is trained with the same five fixed seeds and compared with its matching baseline.

10 configurations × 5 seeds = 50 training runs. Selected comparisons, not a full factorial design.

Pairing, data and reproducibility

Five within-seed differences

For each configuration, compute Δs = mAPconfiguration,s − mAPbaseline,s, then apply a paired t-test (n = 5, df = 4). Report direction consistency alongside the test. Pairing reduces seed-related variation; it does not eliminate training noise.

The head route uses augmented E0. The cached-label route uses E0noaug with geometric augmentation disabled; photometric augmentation remains. Different baselines prevent a direct ranking of the two routes.

Fixed training protocol

YOLO11n · 640 × 640 · 100 epochs · batch 32 · no early stopping. SGD: learning rate 0.01, momentum 0.937, weight decay 0.0005; three warm-up epochs. Seeds 0–4, deterministic mode, NVIDIA V100 32 GB.

Approximately 31,500 training images combine public/private defect images and clean negatives, with private-data oversampling. Scene grouping and pHash deduplication reduce leakage.

Put supervision on the defect.

A thin crack occupies little of its bounding region. I compared three ways of placing the distillation weight, including a polygon mask that follows the crack itself.

A training-time weighting design. The deployed student still detects boxes.

From the illustration to the implemented weights

Uniform

All head locations participate without foreground selection.

Box-derived foreground

gt_positive uses assigner-positive head locations. The rectangular illustration shows the background problem; implementation does not weight every image pixel inside the box.

Mask foreground

gt_seg rasterizes ground-truth polygons and uses max-pooling when reducing masks to each head grid, preserving thin defects. These are supervision weights, not student segmentation predictions.

The box-heavy E1fgbox ablation changes classification ×0.5 and box loss ×2. It is not a box-only loss.

Separate placement from strength.

Foreground weighting also changes the loss magnitude. Raising λ without spatial weighting provides a control for that increase.

The mask variant takes a different route: a public gain over no KD, with less effective strength than standard uniform weighting.

The mask result supports spatially selective supervision. It is not a paired superiority claim over uniform weighting, nor the source of the 17% cross-architecture result.

A gain could come from regularization rather than useful teacher knowledge. The shuffle test retains the extra loss but disrupts image–target correspondence.

Interpretation and limits of the shuffle control

Whole teacher-output bundles are shuffled across images in each batch. The control retains the structured output pool and loss computation, but does not guarantee identical numerical loss or gradient distributions. A permutation can retain some matched items.

Matched cross-architecture targets outperform shuffled targets on both validation domains: public paired Δ +0.043 (p < 0.001), private +0.041 (p < 0.05). This supports a contribution from correctly associated information; it does not exclude every possible regularization mechanism.

The head-route shuffle control uses λ = 0.5. There is no corresponding λ = 1.06 shuffle experiment. Numerical results are available below.

The deployed architecture is unchanged

YOLO11n · 2.6M parameters · 6.6 GFLOPs at 640 × 640 · 5.4 MB model. No teacher or KD-loss computation at inference.

M2 Core ML CPU_AND_NE: 2.2–2.7 ms per prediction across ten seed-0 configurations. FP16, 640 × 640, batch 1, random input, no embedded NMS. Eight warm-up calls and 60 timed model.predict calls; median per run.

This excludes camera capture and application rendering. It is not an iPad end-to-end frame rate or a guarantee of identical latency on every device.

A tested method,
with defined limits.

The contribution is an output-based transfer interface, foreground-weighting design and controlled evaluation—not a claim of maximum detector accuracy.

Gains are modest. Dataset quality and the selected teachers are plausible limits discussed in the thesis; they were not isolated as the sole causes. Evidence covers two classes, one student, two teachers and five seeds per configuration. Broader architecture and domain transfer still require independent evaluation.
